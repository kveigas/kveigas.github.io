01 Annotation quality Python · FastAPI · React

DataQual

Evidence-driven annotation quality, review prioritization and adaptive label collection.

Turns raw multi-annotator labels into agreement statistics with intervals, consensus under different assumptions (majority vote vs. a from-scratch Dawid–Skene worker model), annotator reliability with credible intervals, explainable disagreement flags and a ranked review queue. Its newest module decides, item by item, whether to stop paying for labels, ask for more, or escalate to an expert.

Case study · 01

DataQual

The problem

Annotation teams need to know whether a dataset is reliable enough to use, where disagreement comes from, and where scarce review and labeling budget should go. Too often the answer is a spreadsheet or a single quality number that hides annotator uncertainty, consensus assumptions and the difference between an error and a genuinely ambiguous item.

What I built

Agreement & coverage

Percent agreement, pairwise agreement on genuinely shared items and Krippendorff’s alpha, all with bootstrap intervals.

Consensus

Majority vote, weighted vote and a from-scratch Dawid–Skene EM model, compared side by side with every item where they disagree.

Annotator reliability

Beta-Binomial shrinkage with leave-one-worker-out priors, 95% credible intervals and Dirichlet-smoothed confusion evidence.

Disagreement diagnostics

Explainable rules separate probable defects, ambiguity or policy gaps, mixed evidence and insufficient evidence, each with a recommended action.

Review prioritization

A ranked, paginated queue with a decomposable score, compared against four baselines on a deterministic simulator.

Adaptive label collection

Per-item stop / ask-for-more / escalate decisions from a smoothed worker model, with no gold used for planning.

Validation

  • Reference parity. 100% hard-label parity with the pinned Crowd-Kit Dawid–Skene implementation on a real requirements-annotation benchmark (posterior MAE ≈ 7.7×10⁻¹¹).
  • Adaptive collection. Stopping at 95% confidence matched the accuracy of using every label in all 12 synthetic scenarios, with up to 14% fewer labels at 3–5 labels per item and 25–63% fewer at 7–9 (exploratory).
  • Worker model. Up to +27.4 accuracy points over majority vote when some annotators are adversarial.
  • Engineering. 131 backend tests at 92.2% branch-inclusive coverage, Ruff and Pyright clean, frontend unit tests and a production build in CI.

Product

Stack

Python 3.12 · FastAPI · DuckDB · Parquet · NumPy/SciPy · React · TypeScript · Vite · Pytest · Pyright · Ruff · Vitest · Playwright · axe

Limits I state up front

Synthetic benchmarks with five seeds are not enough for broad claims. The worker model assumes annotators err independently, and its posterior is not a calibrated error probability. The public demo has no authentication: never upload confidential data.

Case study · 02

OpsPilot

The problem

Human-data programs (annotation, RLHF preference ranking, AI evaluation) can fail operationally even when the guidelines are clear. A manager juggles qualification, capacity, routing, production state, review backlogs, escalations, SLA pressure and delivery — usually across dashboards that don’t agree and actions nobody can audit.

What I built

Decision brief

The Today view leads with the next best action, plain-language SLA reasons, a completion forecast and a delivered-quality outlook.

Quality-aware allocation

Work goes only to annotators with every required skill, a passed calibration and capacity on the day; urgent work goes to the strongest QA tier. Each allocation records why.

Adaptive, trust-based QA

A Beta posterior per annotator sets the tier (trusted 5%, standard 20%, probation 50%, at risk 100%). Inclusion probabilities are stored, so quality is estimated with design weights.

Execution & escalations

A 10-state task machine rejects invalid transitions; rework is capped at three attempts, then escalated with a recorded decision.

Forecast & delivery

A Monte Carlo forecast gives P50/P90 dates. Five mandatory delivery gates each show their evidence and a link to fix them.

Operational safeguards

One transaction per business operation, idempotency keys on every mutation, UTC-correct timestamps and a non-destructive demo bootstrap.

Validation

  • Budget-matched benchmark. Over 40 paired synthetic workforces, adaptive QA shipped 1.8% undetected errors versus 3.5% for uniform review at the same budget (better in 40 of 40), and needed 43% fewer reviews to match its quality.
  • Correctness under failure. A failure injected mid-workday leaves tasks, reviews and audit records unchanged.
  • Engineering. 74 backend tests at 95% coverage, Ruff and Pyright clean, 41 frontend unit tests, and Playwright flows with axe accessibility checks on all nine views.

Product

Stack

Python 3.12 · FastAPI · SQLAlchemy 2 · Pydantic 2 · SQLite · React 18 · TypeScript · Vite · Tailwind · Pytest · Vitest · Playwright · axe

Limits I state up front

All data is synthetic. The QA estimators assume reviewers are correct, the forecast has not been backtested on real throughput, and SLA status is rules-based rather than predictive. Authentication, multi-tenancy and hosted backups are not implemented.
